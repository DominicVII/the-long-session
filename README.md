# The Long Session

A first-person political life simulator. You are one member of a legislature in an
invented republic, and the only thing you accumulate is what other people think of you.

**Play:** https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io/the-long-session/

Or open `index.html` from anywhere — a download, a USB stick, a folder on your desktop.
It is one file. There is no build step and nothing to install.

Everything runs in the page. No connector, no account, no permission prompt, nothing
sent anywhere — every word anybody says is written in the game, out of who they are and
what has happened to them. It plays the same with no signal at all.

---

## Getting in

1. **New career** → build your character: frame, dress, skin, hair, height, build,
   glasses, beard, and the **seat you sit for** — any of the twenty states, and your
   district number.
2. Then the six-question sorting test. Your answers *are* your politics: they set your
   position on four axes, your loyalty and your starting standing.
3. Take a party's ticket, sit as an independent, or **found your own party**. Every AI
   member sat the same test; the chamber's composition is the result of it, not a preset.

## Controls

| | |
|---|---|
| **W / S** | walk |
| **A / D** | look left and right |
| **Q / C** | step sideways |
| **Arrows** | look and pitch |
| **Drag** | look, both axes |
| **E** | speak to whoever you face / use what you face |
| **F** | your phone |
| **Hold V** | speak out loud |
| **H** | hide the display |
| **Space** | pause |
| **Shift** | stride (people notice if you barge past them) |

Xbox or any standard gamepad: left stick walks and steps, right stick looks, **A**
speaks or takes the highlighted line, **B** closes, **View** opens your phone, **Start**
pauses, triggers run. The **D-pad** walks every prompt on screen — the suggested lines,
the buttons on any card, the questionnaire, where to go, the panel you are reading.

All of it is rebindable under **Settings**, along with voices and film grain.

## The day

A full day — eight in the morning to ten at night — takes **30 real minutes at 1×**.
2× and 4× scale from there; conversations and actions charge their own minutes on top.

Each morning a card reads you the diary. **Accept** a party meeting or a hearing and you
will be walked in when the hour comes. **Decline** and it costs you less than simply not
turning up.

## Conversations

Everyone you stop wanted something out of their own day, and you cannot get to your
business until you have dealt with theirs. One asks where you are on the bill before
they will hear anything else. One tells you their district is going badly and they will
not win it twice. One warns you their rival has been asking about you. One admits they
have not been home in eleven days.

Apologise properly, explain yourself, or refuse to give ground — each moves warmth,
writes a memory in their voice, and sometimes gets you something. **Push somebody
already sour and they walk off mid-sentence.**

No two are the same. The opening is composed from a stance, whatever is actually on
their mind, and how they are carrying it, then checked against every line the game has
ever put in anybody's mouth. Nobody runs the same thread twice.

## In session

When an event starts and you are in the room, it is not a card. The chair opens it,
members are called in turn and **say their half out loud**, somebody heckles from the
floor, and the argument moves the count while it happens. Everyone with a seat is in it,
turned to whoever is speaking; the rest write, nod, lean across to their neighbour, or
stare at the ceiling.

Then the chair calls your name and you are on your feet with **a clock running** — four
things you could say, or a box to write your own.

**You hear them.** Every character has a voice, consistent for that person, with pitch
and pace off their temperament, and it falls off with distance: a voice across the
chamber is a murmur, the same voice at your elbow is somebody talking to you. Members
standing together confer out loud where you can overhear them.

## Witnesses

The country walks in and sits in a chair. A nurse who drove a woman forty-one miles in
the back of her own car. A widow with a letter from this Congress promising the levee,
dated eleven years ago. A colonel who will answer what the committee is cleared to ask.

Press them hard, give them room, or thank them and let them go. Some unite a room that
has been fighting for a week; some split one that agreed an hour ago. **And a member of
this House can be put in that chair** — to answer for a vote that moved the day after a
donation landed, with you deciding whether to defend them or ask the question nobody
else will.

## Vox

Everyone has an account and none of them have any judgement. Members post all day, back
each other or come at each other in public, and carry it into the corridor afterwards.

You post from your phone — **in your own words**. The game reads what you wrote: who you
named, whether you went at them or backed them, whether it is about the bill or your
district. Reach is a roll weighted by your standing; it carries, does its rounds, or goes
nowhere, and heat travels furthest of all, which is not the same as doing you any good.

## What actually matters

**People remember.** Every favour, threat, broken promise and conversation writes a
signed, decaying memory. Grudges fade three times slower than kindnesses.

**Nobody can read anyone's numbers.** You get a *read*, and the read is wrong in
proportion to how little you know them.

**Everything travels.** Hurt one member and their friends hear. Every private threat
becomes a secret with its own clock.

**Nothing accrues for turning up.**

## Autonomy

Nobody is waiting for you. Every member has a town dying of something specific, a thing
they came here to get, and a thing they are afraid of. When a bill touches any of it,
**what it does to their town outweighs what their party told them that morning** — and
they act on it alone: leaning on colleagues, trading real things for votes, threatening
people, posting about it, going quietly under.

## The republic

- **Money** is the guilder. The index answers to industry, unrest and what the House spends.
- **Twenty states**, each with generated towns, industry and trouble.
- **Bills are drawn out of the country** — a real domain, a real town, that town's actual
  problem, four to six clauses, a cost, who pays and who it helps. Never the same twice.
- **Three branches.** The executive vetoes; two thirds overrides. A court of five can
  strike a bill down.
- **The form of the state follows the chamber** — republic, mass democracy, conservative
  order, people's commonwealth, national state, elective monarchy, empire.
- You start at the **1st Congress**, so there are no predecessors.

## The Record

An encyclopaedia in seven parts, all of it generated by the career you are having: the
Republic, the line of presidents, the states and their towns, the parties and the rows
they were founded in, everyone who serves and everyone who served, the statute book, and
the things you have found in the building.

Press any name anywhere — a member, a state, a party, the Congress number, the bill — and
it explains itself.

## Failing

Lose your seat and you go to the East Steps as a private citizen. Win people over and you
can stand again. **Lose twice and that is the career.**

## Odds and ends

- **Save** at any moment; it also autosaves every 20 game-minutes and at every room change.
- **Hide the display** leaves the view, whoever is speaking and the wire. One corner
  button brings it back.
- **Letters, plaques and tablets** are scattered through the building and accumulate in
  your record.
- **Add to Home Screen** installs it with its own icon, full screen.

## Running it yourself

Nothing to install. Three ways, in order of least effort:

1. **GitHub Pages** — Settings → Pages → Source: `main`, folder `/ (root)`. A minute later it is
   live at `https://YOUR-USERNAME.github.io/the-long-session/` and stays live.
2. **Open the file** — double-click `index.html`. Saving works in most browsers; a few block
   local storage on `file://`, in which case the game runs but will not remember your career.
3. **Any static host** — drop `index.html` anywhere that serves files.

The only thing it fetches is three.js from a CDN and two fonts from Google. Everything else —
every texture, every face, every garment, every line anybody says — is made in code as you play.
Your career is saved in your own browser and goes nowhere else.

## If it runs badly

**Settings → Graphics → Simple.** Same game, far less asked of the machine. A room with more
than six people in it switches to that on its own and switches back when it empties.
