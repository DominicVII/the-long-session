## The Engine learns

## The nature of things

`20261017q` (on `20261017p`). Keeps NEPTUNE-UNIFORMS and NEPTUNE-CLARITY verbatim; `public/session.html` keeps its `../neptune/` path.

- **The world has a nature of its own,** fixed for each game and unknown to everyone in it: whether closed timelike curves are allowed, whether the past can be changed, which reading of quantum mechanics holds, whether consciousness is fundamental, whether a copied mind is the same person, whether other universes exist, whether there is one theory of everything, and about forty more questions across quantum physics, time and spacetime, the cosmos, thermodynamics, information, mind and life.
- **People and the Engine investigate it.** A question is taken up once what it stands on has been found. Experiments lean toward the truth, with noise, and only as well as the work and instruments allow. The Republic comes to believe an answer, and can be wrong: a better experiment, or a prototype that fails because its premise was false, overturns it.
- **Inventions follow belief.** A quantum network, a fault-tolerant quantum computer, a vacuum energy tap, time-dilation couriers, a closed timelike channel, a wormhole link, a warp craft, mind transfer, a test for machine consciousness and more are built only when the world allows them. A time channel is either self-consistent (the past can only be told what it already knew) or branching (each use leaves a world behind), depending on that world's nature.
- **There is no end to the questions.** Settled results combine into new ones, and every real anomaly in the instruments raises a question about the law behind it, with no ceiling on depth. What is found also feeds the Engine's emergent behaviour.
- All of it is on the Academy card under *The nature of things*, and the people who work these fields talk about what they are testing.

## Kin: the families nobody sees

`20261015k` (on `20261015j`). Keeps NEPTUNE-UNIFORMS and NEPTUNE-CLARITY verbatim; `public/session.html` keeps its `../neptune/` path.

They are never in the room.
- **It moves votes.** A relative's trade pulls on how someone reads a bill (a spouse who owns the mill, a sibling who is a union steward, a mother who needs care push toward or away from provision, a family on old money toward the market, a struggling one toward the public). It adds to the existing reading of a bill, so two members of one party can split because of who they go home to.
- **It decides who can run.** A name that carries, money, a supportive spouse, no one to look after: higher. A frail parent, a sick child, small children, a spouse who does not want it, a sibling's debts: lower. This feeds into how well members do at the polls and into who becomes your rival. - **It shows up in talk.** When you talk to someone they may mention home, in their own words (a frail mother, a child's school report, an estranged sibling). The live-chat dossier includes their family and its strains.
- **Life goes on in other people's houses:** births, weddings, divorces, deaths and illness happen to people each day; the ones involving people you know show up in the feed and the Ledger, and are remembered by the person.
- **It touches the purse.** A spouse's pay, children's costs and a parent's care change what each person has left each day, you included.
- A new Apps entry, The families, lists who is behind you and behind the members you know, with their strains and how able they are to run.

## The situation room for governors and Congress's levers

## The situation room: what a leader may do about a neighbour

`20261015i` (on `20261015h`). Keeps NEPTUNE-UNIFORMS and NEPTUNE-CLARITY verbatim; `public/session.html` keeps its `../neptune/` path.

- **Leaders can act on a quarrel, not just talk:** as President you get a situation room (on the daily docket, and as a 'Take it further' button on a neighbour's page on the map). With the Reach holding the bread, the options run from a formal protest, recalling your ambassador and buying grain from their rivals, through sanctions, escorting the convoys and quietly funding their opposition, to a blockade, seizing the grain depots, calling the levy and declaring war. Every option says plainly what it does, who gains, who loses and what the risk is.
- **The law decides what you may do alone:** the form of the state (an empire or monarchy lets the executive do more; a mass democracy lets it do less) and the power precedents already set raise or lower how far you may go without Congress. Past that line you can ask Congress (it answers in two days, and an authorisation lasts thirty days), or act first and answer for it: Congress may vote a retroactive authorisation, or censure you and send the question to the bench. Acting first sets a precedent that makes the next time easier, and pushes the state toward a different form.
- **Nobody is told what to think of it:** before you choose, you can see how the House reads it, how every state backs it from its own temper (creeds, industry, bread prices, unrest), and how each neighbour reads it from its own interests and rivalries: a rival of the Reach may back you quietly, the Horn League may object to warships, the Free Marches to soldiers on a frontier. After you act, the chambers, the states, the neighbours and the target each answer the next morning.
- **And the time comes:** measures build pressure. After a few days it resolves: the target concedes and bread gets cheaper, nothing is settled, or it backfires with dearer bread, dead to explain and the states that never backed it saying so. It depends on the pressure you applied, how the country backed it, who stood with you and who against, and a good deal of luck.
