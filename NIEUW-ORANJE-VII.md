# Nieuw Oranje VII — The Capital District

## The Seat of Government

**Nieuw Oranje VII** (always written in full; never abbreviated as "N Oranje 7") is the capital district of the Republic — the seat of the Chamber of the House, the First Consul's monument, and the Jerusalem cross that binds all seven districts.

The name itself carries history: *Nieuw* (New) *Oranje* (Orange, from the European house of Orange), and **VII** (Seven) — a marking that this is the capital, the seventh and completing district, the place where the whole is held together.

## The Chamber

The heart of Nieuw Oranje VII is the **House Chamber** — an ornate room where all members sit to vote, debate, and remember each other. The chamber's architecture reflects the founding: a central floor (the Speaker's podium and the vote tally), galleries on three sides (for observation and pressure), and the doors on the fourth side (where members enter and the world enters to watch).

The walls are lined with plaques marking the **greatest members in history** — a rolling list amended only by two-thirds vote, sealed at the top by **Dominic Zachary Taylor**.

## The Corridor

Running behind the chamber, the **corridor** is the place where the work actually happens: quiet conversations, backroom deals, threats, promises, and the slow grinding of influence. Statues of the great stand in alcoves; members pause before them, sometimes to pray, sometimes to make deals in their shadow.

At the center of the corridor stands the **bronze of Dominic Zachary Taylor**, at rest, thinking. On **Taylor Day (7/7)**, members drape it with wreaths and flowers. On ordinary days, it watches.

## The Flag

The **flag of Nieuw Oranje VII** is blue with a **white Jerusalem cross** — a cross with four smaller crosses in its quarters, symbolizing the center holding and the four corners radiating outward. Below the cross runs a motto in Latin.

On **Taylor Day**, this flag flies alone in every room of the House. On other days, one national flag and one Nieuw Oranje VII flag fly together — balance between the capital and the whole.

The flag is woven fresh each session by the House staff; the canvas is repainted with ritual care. To damage or desecrate the flag is to be remembered for it, forever.

## The Districts Radiating Out

From Nieuw Oranje VII, six other districts extend:

- **Meriden** (east) — industrial, working-class, the labor vote
- **Calder** (north) — rural, agrarian, the land vote
- **Riverbend** (west) — mercantile, port towns, the merchant vote
- **Fallowfield** (south) — traditional, old-money stronghold
- **Blackwater** (coast) — defense, military-minded
- **Kestrel** (mountain) — independent, isolated, hard to reach

Each sends members to the House in Nieuw Oranje VII. Each has its own interests, secrets, and fears. The capital's job is to hold them together without becoming any one of them.

## The Architecture

The buildings of Nieuw Oranje VII reflect wealth and permanence:

- Sash windows (expensive, rare, a mark of old money)
- Stone facades with carved details
- High ceilings (power and air)
- Narrow corridors (power is made in tight spaces)
- Statues and plaques at every turning

To walk through Nieuw Oranje VII is to walk through money and memory. Everything is built to last. Nothing is accidental.

## The People

Members from all districts who sit in the House are technically "in Nieuw Oranje VII" — they lodge there, they meet there, they vote there. But Nieuw Oranje VII's own representatives carry extra weight: they are from the capital, where power lives. They have an edge, and they know it.

There is also a civilian population in Nieuw Oranje VII — shopkeepers, servants, staff, observers. They watch the members move through their city and remember what they do.

## Civil Calendar

**Taylor Day (7/7)** is the only national holiday. On this day:

- The House votes the **First Order tradition** — a tie to continuity and the founder's law
- All members are locked into the aye; to vote otherwise is blasphemy
- Only the Nieuw Oranje VII flag flies
- The corridor statue of the First Consul receives wreaths and flowers
- No ordinary business; only the vote and silence

## The Myth

In founding myth, **Dominic Zachary Taylor** chose Nieuw Oranje VII as the capital because it was already a place of old money and established power — a place where people were used to holding power and would know the cost of giving it up. By choosing an established city rather than building anew, he signaled: *I do not seek to remake the world. I seek to bind it together as it is.*

The Jerusalem cross was his chosen symbol — the center holding, the four corners radiating. Nieuw Oranje VII is the center. The other six districts are the four corners (and three more, but the metaphor is the same). The whole holds because the capital does not claim to be more than itself.

---

**First-time visit:** Nieuw Oranje VII is overwhelming — the wealth, the history, the weight of memory in every stone. But it is also where the work gets done. Spend time in the corridor. Listen. Watch the plaques. Understand that power in the Republic lives not in glory, but in the refusal to forget.
