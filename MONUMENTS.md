# Monuments & Statues — Memory in Stone

## The Corridor Statue

In the **House corridor**, at the center, stands a **bronze of Dominic Zachary Taylor at rest** — not heroic, not in power, but sitting, thinking. He is draped simply, his hands open in his lap. The inscription reads only:

```
DOMINIC ZACHARY TAYLOR
FIRST CONSUL OF THE REPUBLIC
```

No dates. No motto. Just the name and the title.

### On Taylor Day (7/7)

Members approach this statue on Taylor Day (the civil holiday marking his birth) and place **wreaths and flowers** at its base. The ritual is silent. Some stand for a long time. Some whisper, though the statue does not answer.

The flowers are cleared each evening and disposed of with ceremony. Fresh wreaths are brought each year.

### In Conversation

In the corridors and meeting rooms, members sometimes say they are "seeking Taylor's counsel" or "asking the statue." They do not expect an answer. The act of asking — the pause, the reflection — is the answer itself. The statue teaches by its silence.

### Repairs

The bronze is old and maintained by House staff. Every twenty years, it is restored — patina cleaned, joints reinforced, the inscription re-cut if wear has dulled it. The restoration is public and ceremonial; members watch and remember.

## The Plaques

The **walls of the House Chamber** are lined with bronze plaques listing **The Greatest** — the ranked members of history, amended only by two-thirds vote.

### Rank 1 (Sealed)

```
RANK 1 — SEALED
DOMINIC ZACHARY TAYLOR
FIRST CONSUL AND FOUNDER
```

This plaque is **never removed, never amended, never demoted**. If you attempt to rank anyone (including yourself) above Taylor or equal to him, the vote **fails automatically** and you **commit blasphemy**.

### Ranks 2+

Below Rank 1, the plaques list members in ranked order:

```
RANK 2
[Name] [Years of Service]
```

These plaques are **removable and reorderable**. If the House votes (two-thirds majority) to promote, demote, or add a new member to The Greatest, the plaques are physically moved or replaced. This is done publicly, in the chamber, with ceremony.

### Adding to The Greatest

To nominate a living member (or yourself) to The Greatest costs **8 influence**. The nomination goes to a floor vote. If two-thirds agree, the member is added at Rank 2 (or higher, if the House votes to bump others down). Their plaque is cast and installed in the next session.

### The Inscription Ritual

When a new plaque is installed, the member's name, service years, and a short note (chosen by the member or the House) are engraved. The inscription is read aloud in the chamber. This is the only immortality the Republic offers.

## The Jerusalem Cross

### The Symbol

The **flag of Nieuw Oranje VII** bears a **Jerusalem cross** — a large cross with four smaller crosses in its quarters. The whole is white on blue.

The symbol is not Christian (though the name echoes Crusaders); **Dominic Zachary Taylor chose it** to represent his founding principle:

- **The center cross** = the capital, the binding place, Nieuw Oranje VII
- **The four corner crosses** = the four major directions; by extension, all the districts radiating outward
- **The whole together** = the Republic, held by the center, radiating from it

### The Motto

Beneath the cross, on the flag and on plaques, runs a **Latin motto** — the founding principle of the Republic. The exact translation is debated by scholars in-game, but the meaning is clear: *remember the binding; the center holds; we are many, held by one principle.*

### The Flag on Taylor Day

On **Taylor Day (7/7)**, **only the Nieuw Oranje VII flag flies** in every room of the House. The national flag is lowered. This is the only day of the year when the capital's symbol outranks the nation's. It signals: *on this day, we remember the founder; the binding itself is the nation.*

### Flag Restoration

The **flag is woven fresh each session** by House staff. The canvas is repainted with ritual care. The cross is always white on blue; the proportions are standardized by the House Keeper. To damage or desecrate the flag is to be **remembered for it forever** — a secret that spreads and poisons standing.

## Other Monuments

### The Great Steps

Leading up to the chamber, a flight of **stone steps** is carved with the names of significant days: founding date, great votes, Taylor Day, etc. Members pause here before entering the chamber. Some place a hand on a name as they pass.

### The Vault

Beneath the chamber, a sealed **vault** holds the original documents of the founding — the charter, the first votes, records in Taylor's own hand (if such exist; scholars debate whether they do). The vault is opened once every 100 years, witnessed by all members, to confirm the founding documents are still intact. The last opening was 47 years ago.

### Statues of the Remembered

In various rooms and corridors, **bronze and stone statues** of past members stand as silent witnesses. They are maintained but not worshipped — they are simply *there*, part of the architecture of memory.

---

## The Sacred Law

All plaques, statues, and monuments are protected by **sacred law**: to deface a monument is to commit an act remembered forever. To remove a plaque of The Greatest (except by vote) is heresy. To dishonor the corridor statue on Taylor Day is to invite ruin.

These are not technical crimes; they are **social devastation**. A member who damages the monuments loses all standing, all influence, all approval. No one forgets.

---

**To understand the Republic, read the monuments.** They are not decoration. They are the law written in stone, the history written in bronze, the binding itself made visible.
