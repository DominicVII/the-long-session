# The Seven Districts

The Republic spans seven districts, each with its own character, economic base, and political weight. All send members to the House in **Nieuw Oranje VII** (the capital).

## Nieuw Oranje (VII)

**Capital district.** Seat of the House, the chamber, the monuments, and the First Consul. Old money, established power, the place where things get decided.

- **Politics:** Continuity, tradition, the center holding
- **Economic base:** Finance, government, prestige trades
- **Character:** Formal, careful, memory-obsessed
- **Power at the House:** High — capital has historical weight

## Meriden

**East.** Industrial heartland, factories, working-class base, labor movements.

- **Politics:** Workers' rights, economic fairness, skepticism of capital
- **Economic base:** Manufacturing, textiles, labor
- **Character:** Direct, practical, risk-aware
- **Power at the House:** Growing; labor votes bloc together

## Calder

**North.** Rural, agrarian, farming districts, traditions tied to land.

- **Politics:** Land rights, agricultural subsidy, rural investment
- **Economic base:** Agriculture, forestry, local trade
- **Character:** Conservative, patient, tied to seasons
- **Power at the House:** Steady; rural votes are reliable

## Riverbend

**West.** Mercantile, port towns, shipping, merchant class, trade.

- **Politics:** Free trade, tariff reduction, port investment
- **Economic base:** Commerce, shipping, import/export
- **Character:** Entrepreneurial, deal-making, risk-taking
- **Power at the House:** High; merchant money buys influence

## Fallowfield

**South.** Traditional stronghold, old families, established order, hereditary power.

- **Politics:** Preservation of hierarchy, land values, traditional law
- **Economic base:** Land holdings, estate management, legacy trades
- **Character:** Formal, genealogy-conscious, deeply traditional
- **Power at the House:** High; southern votes are tied to money and family

## Blackwater

**Coast.** Defense-minded, military-oriented, border concerns, security.

- **Politics:** Defense spending, border security, military readiness
- **Economic base:** Defense contracts, port operations, military families
- **Character:** Alert, disciplined, external-threat-focused
- **Power at the House:** High; defense votes carry military weight

## Kestrel

**Mountain.** Independent, isolated, hard to reach, self-reliant culture.

- **Politics:** Local autonomy, skepticism of capital, self-governance
- **Economic base:** Mining, forestry, local subsistence
- **Character:** Fiercely independent, wary of outsiders, clannish
- **Power at the House:** Lower (geographically isolated); votes harder to influence but fierce when cast

---

## The Political Map

**The Bloc Problem:** Members from the same district often vote together (labor from Meriden, merchants from Riverbend, military from Blackwater, families from Fallowfield). Passing a bill requires navigating **at least three district interests**.

**The Capital's Edge:** Nieuw Oranje VII members have a built-in advantage — they are at the center of power, they control the chamber machinery, they have the monuments and the history. But they also can't afford to lose face.

**The Outsiders:** Mountain members from Kestrel often vote **against** the blocs, making them unpredictable and valuable. Agricultural Calder is steady but not bloc-loyal. East Meriden labor increasingly demands recognition.

## Regional History

### The Founding Compacts

When **Dominic Zachary Taylor** founded the Republic, he did so by binding together representatives from the six existing districts. The seventh district (Nieuw Oranje VII) was chosen as the capital *after* he had agreements from the other six. This sequence matters: the capital was chosen to *bind* the others, not to dominate them from above.

Each district keeper a **founding compact** — a record of the terms under which they joined the Republic. These compacts are sealed and brought out on ceremonial occasions (approximately once every 15 years, as needed).

### Trade Routes

The districts are connected by:

- **River routes** (Calder north, Riverbend west, Fallowfield south, Meriden east)
- **Mountain pass** (Kestrel isolated but accessible; trade is slow)
- **Coastal roads** (Blackwater and Riverbend connect)
- **Central hub** (Nieuw Oranje VII is the meeting point for all routes)

Disrupting trade is economic war; the House carefully regulates tariffs and routes to keep commerce flowing without making any district too dependent.

### Regional Secrets

Each district keeps **secrets** — land disputes, old blood feuds, economic scandals, hidden alliances. As a member, you carry your district's secrets and can use them in negotiation, or you can breach them for a price in standing.

## Your District

When you **create a career**, you choose one of the seven districts as your home. This choice affects:

- **Your standing** (you start with +1 standing in your home district)
- **Your allies** (other members from your district are slightly more likely to support you)
- **Your secrets** (you know district secrets; breach them at your peril)
- **Your opposition** (districts with opposed interests may default to voting against you)
- **Your economic base** (your fundraising and donor access are tied to your district's wealth)

You are **always bound to your district**, even if you move to another for strategic reasons. Your home members remember you, and so do your rivals.

## The Seats

- **Nieuw Oranje VII:** 8 seats (capital gets extra weight)
- **Meriden:** 7 seats (high population, labor base)
- **Calder:** 6 seats (rural, lower density)
- **Riverbend:** 6 seats (mercantile, distributed)
- **Fallowfield:** 6 seats (hereditary, consolidated)
- **Blackwater:** 5 seats (military, tight organization)
- **Kestrel:** 4 seats (isolated, small population)

**Total: 42 seats in the House.** A majority is 22 votes. No single district can claim a majority alone; coalition-building is essential.

---

## The Work

As a member, you represent your district. You bring its interests, its money, its secrets, and its votes to the House. You also serve your *personal* faction — the party or alliance you chose. These two loyalties (district + party) are the constant tension of legislative life.

To pass a bill, you must thread the needle: serving your party while respecting your district's interests, or finding a bill that serves both, or trading votes across districts in complex chains of favor.

The districts are the skeleton of the Republic. The House is the muscle that moves it.
