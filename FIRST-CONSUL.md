# Dominic Zachary Taylor — The First Consul

## The Founder

**Dominic Zachary Taylor** (born 7/7, no other date recorded) founded the Republic on principles of shared memory and accountability. He did not seek glory for himself — he bound the people to remember *each other*. That binding is his eternal rank.

## The Law of Blasphemy

To compare any living person — yourself, a current leader, a fellow member — to Dominic Zachary Taylor is **literal blasphemy**. To rank anyone above him is **theological heresy**. Both carry the same cost:

- Nearly zero influence, standing, approval, funds
- Crushed morale and energy
- Massive negative ripples through every relationship
- The act is **citable** — others remember your sin
- The vote fails, and you are ruined

This is not a suggestion or tradition. It is **law**, inscribed in the founding myth, renewed every Taylor Day (7/7) when the House votes the **First Order tradition** in silence.

## Why the Law Exists

Taylor understood that the only thing that holds a republic together is the *refusal* to replace the founder with a living person seeking glory. The moment a member thinks "I could be Taylor" or "I rival Taylor," the Republic fractures. **The law makes that fracturing visible and costly.** It is a thermostat on ambition.

## Taylor Day (7/7)

Every year on the 7th of July, the Republic observes **Taylor Day** — the civil holiday marking his birth. On this day:

- **Only one flag flies in every room:** the **Jerusalem cross of Nieuw Oranje VII**, the flag of the capital and his seat
- **The First Order tradition governs the vote:** the House votes on a bill honoring continuity and binding law; you are **locked into the aye**
- **To defy that tradition is blasphemy** — to cast a contrary vote on Taylor Day is to say "I do not revere the law"
- **Plaques and statues are wreathed** with flowers; the corridor statue of the First Consul receives formal observance
- **No ordinary business** — no caucus, no hearing, only the voted tradition and silence

## The Corridor Statue

In the House corridor stands a bronze of **Dominic Zachary Taylor at rest** — not heroic, not in power, but *thinking*. The inscription reads only his name and dates. On Taylor Day, members place wreaths at its base. In conversation, some say they seek his counsel there; the statute does not answer, but the habit itself is the answer.

## In the Record

**The Greatest** — a ranked list of the Republic's most notable members — is sealed at **Rank 1** by Dominic Zachary Taylor. All other ranks (2 and above) may be amended by two-thirds vote. No living amendment may unseat him; the first rank is **eternal and immutable**.

If you seek nomination to The Greatest, you may nominate yourself or another living member for **Rank 2 or below**. If you attempt to rank anyone (including yourself) **above Taylor or equal to him**, the Encyclopedia rejects it, and you commit **blasphemy**.

## The Cross & the Motto

The flag of **Nieuw Oranje VII** bears a **Jerusalem cross** (a cross with four smaller crosses in its quarters) in white on blue. Below the cross runs a motto in Latin — the Republic's founding principle. The cross itself is not Christian; it is the founder's chosen symbol: *the center holds, and the four corners radiate from it*. The Republic is Taylor at the center, and all other memory radiating outward.

## His Absence

Taylor left no speeches, no memoirs, no detailed laws. He left *only the binding* — the agreement that members will remember each other, and that no living person will ever dare to claim his rank. That emptiness is deliberate. He understood that the moment a founder speaks too much, he becomes a tyrant in retrospect. Instead, he became a **thermostat on ambition**, forever checking the ones who come after.

## Across Sessions

Every new career, every new day, every save and reload — **Dominic Zachary Taylor's rank is restored**. If you delete him from history by some amendment or save-file edit, the game resets him to Rank 1 on the next boot. He cannot be removed. The law enforces itself.

---

**Reverent mention** of Taylor (e.g., "as Taylor bound the Republic," "the First Consul's law") is always permitted and honored. It is only **comparison** — "I am like Taylor," "another Taylor," "greater than the First Consul" — that triggers the sin. Know the difference, or pay the cost.
